Inside the lift: every part, and how it moves

A to-scale 3D model of the lift that sits under the tee box: clear tube, plunger, linear rail, toothed belt, NEMA17 motor, the two gate solenoids and the sensors. Press play to watch one ball go from the funnel to the tray, or pull the exploded-view slider to separate the parts.

Waiting

Parts · click to highlight

    1 · Load

    Plunger waits at the bottom. The lower gate solenoid pulls its pin for 0.15 s and one ball rolls from the funnel channel through the side window onto the plunger face.

    Beam sensor confirms: exactly one ball
    2 · Lift

    The motor turns the lower pulley; the belt pulls the carriage up the rail, and the arm through the tube slot carries the plunger 232 mm up.

    ≈2 s · ~0.1 N·m · 260 rpm peak
    3 · Tip out

    The face is tilted 10° toward the exit. As its low edge clears the wedge lip the tube wall is gone, and gravity rolls the ball out. The roof rises toward the exit, so if the ball jumps it is pushed out too.

    ≈0.2 s to the lip · 0.26 m/s
    4 · Roll & reset

    The ball drops 22 mm down the wedge (≈0.56 m/s) and rolls to the back of the tray. The plunger goes back down; the upper gate lets the next ball into the gap between the gates.

    One ball ≈ every 5 s until the tray is full

    Drive change: toothed belt instead of lead screw

    With the hood, the plunger has to travel 232 mm, and everything must fit under the plywood (about 272 mm clear). A lead screw needs its nut travel plus a bearing block at each end plus a pulley: about 287 mm, which does not fit. A GT2 belt runs on pulleys beside the rail, so only the rail length counts: 232 mm travel + 29 mm carriage = 261 mm. It fits, runs slower (≈260 rpm), and is simpler to service. This replaces the lead screw in the Issue 2 spec.

    Lead screw: 287 mm needed
    GT2 belt: 261 mm needed
    Black line = 272 mm available under the plywood
